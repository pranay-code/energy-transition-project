## Where the India work starts

### `india-power-snapshot-2026.md` — the verified India power numbers ⭐
**Ten sections. Everything the India power argument rests on, from primary sources.** Installed
capacity as on 30 June 2026 (548,858 MW · **54.18% non-fossil**) · the 2025 generation mix
(coal 70.8% · non-fossil 26.6%) · **grid carbon intensity flat at ~0.71 tCO₂/MWh for five years
while renewables grew 73%** · peak demand now **270,832 MW, arriving at 22:36** · storage at
**5.9 of the 411 GWh** needed by 2031-32 · **one-third of new renewables on a temporary grid
connection**, Rajasthan curtailing up to 80% · 509,603 ckm of lines built and ₹9.15 lakh crore
planned · a record **54.6 GW of RE added in FY 2025-26** alongside **~97 GW of new coal planned**.
Holds the fact the whole India argument turns on: **54% of capacity is clean, but only 27% of the
electricity is** — and India's own plan expects 68% capacity but 49% generation by 2031-32.
**Also carries a correction:** the 841 gCO₂-e/kWh grid figure in `module-1C` and `module-1E` is
wrong; use ~710 g/kWh. Refresh commands, and the working Brave Search recipe, at the foot.


### `india-storage-and-grid-flexibility-2026.md` — what actually closes the evening gap ⭐
**Companion to the power snapshot.** That file says how big the problem is; this one says what
fixes it, how fast, at what cost, and who is already doing it. From the **IIT Bombay "Advanced
BESS" short course, 6–7 April 2026** plus the IndiGrid and BMS decks handed out there.
Holds: **Grid-India's two-directional reserve failure on 26 May 2025** — nothing left to turn
*down* for five mid-day hours, nothing left to turn *up* for three night hours, the sharpest
evidence that the constraint is time of day · the matching IEX price curve · **inertia and
grid-forming inverters, a physical challenge nowhere else in this library** · the full storage
technology comparison and why every Indian grid project is LFP · **degradation and why Indian
heat makes it worse** · IndiGrid's real project pipeline and the **18-month build clock** that
gives the solution ranking a sourced basis · costs down 80% and Indian batteries profitable
post-2024 · the policy stack (₹18,100 cr PLI, 40% VGF, 4% storage obligation) and state targets
(AP 25 GWh, Rajasthan 10 GWh) · **CAISO at 42.8% of demand on batteries at 7 pm** as the finished
picture · a JRC/IEA cross-check putting power at **48% of India's CO₂**.
**⚠️ Read §0 first** — the IITB deck restricts third-party sharing, so cite public originals.

## Where the payoff work starts

### `energy-group-asset-map-2026.md` — who the companies are and how they earn ⭐
**The lookup file. Read this before anything else about Energy Group.** Full company names and what each
one does · **the two-layer structure** (listed companies versus divisions of AEL — which decides
whether a transfer is a management decision or a related-party transaction) · **how each company
actually makes money**, which is where most of the opportunities live: a transmission line earns a
**fixed charge for 35 years whether power flows or not** · a thermal plant earns a **capacity
charge just for being available** · smart metering is **4.7 crore monthly annuities** · and the
fact that explains ATGL's behaviour — **CNG and industry pay for the network, so household piped
gas is a licence obligation, not a profit centre**. Then the verified footprint as at 8 Aug 2026,
and a full glossary of the terms (MDO, CGD, GA, CBG, FDRE, AMISP, HAM, TR…).
**Carries the payoff's headline number:** Energy Group's **3.37 GWh at Khavda is 57% of every grid battery
in India**, at one site, built in 10 months against an 18-month industry clock.
⚠️ Company self-disclosure throughout. §6 lists what is unverified.

### `energy-group-opportunity-analysis-2026.md` — the five opportunities, stress-tested ⭐
**The reasoning layer behind the payoff question.** A six-axis rubric where **Energy Group edge decides
everything**, then five opportunities read out of the sector challenges: **the evening hour** ·
**piped gas into low-refill districts** · **waste into a pipe Energy Group already owns** · **the freight
corridor, not the charger** · **ash, cement and the coming shortage**. Plus what to decline, and
the honest coal tension.
**⚠️ Read §5 and §6 before quoting the first two drafts of anything.** Three claims failed
arithmetic and were rebuilt: the LPG subsidy case is modest, not decisive · removing the cascade
truck improves biogas economics but does not fix them, and **sewage sludge is a poor feedstock, so
the STP is the host and food waste is the fuel** · and the grinding-at-the-power-plant logic was
**backwards** — it triples outbound tonnage on the heaviest leg. In two of the three, the honest
version is a *better* argument: the **poorest districts are the commercially superior target**
because only they create new demand, and **Energy Group Cement inherits secure fly ash exactly when
competitors lose theirs**, because Energy Group Power outlives their suppliers.

## Learning modules

### `module-1E-synthesis.md` — the whole argument on one page ⭐
The ten realities grouped into four themes, the four ideas that actually run the report, the
25 challenges by domain, the honest bottom line, and the bridge into the India work.
Ends with a 10-question self-test.
**The four core ideas:** the capacity–generation asymmetry · the transition is lumpy, not
continuous · electricity is not a molecule · the grid is the binding constraint.

### `module-1A-foundations.md` — framing and the starting position
- The **7-domain framework**: Power (hub) → 3 end-use sectors (Mobility, Industry, Buildings)
  → 3 enablers (Raw materials, Energy carriers, Carbon & energy reduction)
- **Five beneficial properties** of fossil energy (energy dense, transportable, dispatchable,
  high heat, chemically flexible) and **two flaws** (~2/3 of energy wasted; >85% of global CO₂)
- **Reality 2:** roughly **10% deployed** against 2050 needs, very unevenly — power ~8–12%,
  EV stock ~3%, heat pumps ~15%, hydrogen <1%, CCUS <1%
- The McKinsey 2023 Achieved Commitments scenario (~1.6 °C) used as a yardstick, not a forecast

### `module-1B-power-and-peaks.md` — power system metamorphosis, and peaks
- **Reality 3:** capacity must **quintuple** by 2050 and go >90% low-emissions. The Germany case:
  **capacity ~3× while generation only ~1.8×** → utilisation falls **27 percentage points**
- Why that one asymmetry breaks the merchant energy-only business model, and forces money to
  move from **energy to availability** (capacity markets, ancillary services, resource adequacy)
- Thermal capacity stays roughly **flat** — its *job* changes from baseload to backup
- Why the grid must almost **triple** in volume (IEA), and why it is the binding constraint
- **Reality 4:** electrified heat raises peaks — US 1.7×, ISO-New England ~3.2×; heat pump COP
  **halves** from 5 °C to −10 °C. Four mitigations, all aimed at flattening the peak
- **India hook:** the peak is inverted — summer-evening, cooling-driven; the solar sunset ramp

### `module-1C-evs-heat-and-turnover.md` — EVs, industrial heat, and turnover windows
- **Reality 5:** grid carbon intensity decides everything. EU ~275, US ~429, global ~529,
  China ~658, **India ~841 gCO₂-e/kWh (2022)**. On a frozen Indian grid a BEV may never break
  even; on a decarbonising path it saves up to 15%. *"An EV is not a clean product — it is a
  claim on the grid."*
- **Reality 6:** the big four (steel, cement, plastics, ammonia) = ~2/3 of industrial emissions.
  49% of industrial energy is >500 °C. Two separate barriers — **heat** (an energy problem) and
  **feedstock** (a chemistry problem). The heat-transfer-form barrier. Aluminium as counterexample
- **Reality 7:** ~**60% of the world's blast furnaces need relining before 2030**.
  *"Timing beats superiority."* The asset-life ladder from cars to buildings
- **India hooks:** rebuild the EV analysis for 2W/3W; India is greenfield in steel

### `module-1D-ccus-hydrogen-minerals.md` — the enablers
- **Reality 8:** CCUS cost scales inversely with CO₂ concentration — ~95–100% for gas processing
  and ethanol, ~21% cement, ~26% steel, ~14% coal power, ~4% gas power. Dilute capture is
  **3–4× costlier** (~$65–75/t vs ~$20/t). *Corrected 11 Aug 2026 — the old `<5 / <10 / ~35 / >50`
  ladder read Exhibit 40's emission-share bubbles as concentrations*
- **Reality 9:** hydrogen loses **40–75%** of its energy. BEV 80–90% vs FCEV 25–35%.
  *"Move the product, not the hydrogen."*
- **Reality 10:** minerals are a **lead-time** problem, not a reserves problem. Lithium ~7× by
  2030; ~40% of lithium demand unmet in the base case; top-3 countries >50% of extraction
- **The full list of all 25 physical challenges**, by domain

### `module-1F-hardstuff-2025-update.md` — the Nov-2025 progress scorecard
- **13.5% deployed vs 17.2% at "cruising speed"** → running at ~half the required pace
- Per-domain annual additions vs requirement (power 0.6/1 TW, EVs 17/58 M, CCUS 2.5/172 Mt…)
- **China = ~2/3 of everything.** Global averages now largely describe China
- **India added >20 GW solar+wind in H1 2025 — more than the US**
- The grid is the only Level 1/2 challenge scored *negative*: **1.65 TW awaiting connection**
- Four wins (batteries, electric trucks, cement CCUS, clean firm power) and four setbacks
  (hydrogen, steel, heat pumps, DAC)

## Q&A notes — questions raised and answered along the way

### `qa-01-feasibility-buyers-and-who-pays.md`
Why 10% deployed isn't as hopeless as it sounds (stock vs flow; ~8.6% CAGR needed) ·
nobody buys a billion *extra* EVs — the world sells ~2bn cars by 2050 anyway, the question is
what share is electric · the three payers: household TCO, taxpayer subsidy, and
mandates-and-standards (the real lever).

### `qa-02-ccus-and-hydrogen.md`
Is CCUS just prolonging fossil reliance? (yes for power/EOR, no for cement's calcination CO₂) ·
where CO₂ actually gets stored, including the **Deccan Traps** option for India · the four cost
layers of CCUS and the "no saleable product" problem · what hydrogen is genuinely for ·
why hydrogen cars lost.

### `qa-03-steel-and-coal.md`
Steelmakers are not forced to use coal by technology — H₂-DRI works. They are blocked by four
things: hydrogen cost, DR-grade ore scarcity, firm clean power, and inability to pass cost
through against 600 Mt of overcapacity. Plus why India's position is structurally better.

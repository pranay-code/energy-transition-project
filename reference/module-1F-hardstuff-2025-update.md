# Module 1F — "The hard stuff 2025": what changed in two years

Source: MGI, *The hard stuff 2025: Taking stock of progress on the physical challenges of the
energy transition*, November 2025 (28 pp). Sequel to the report covered in Modules 1A–1E.
Same 7 domains, same 25 challenges — this one is a **scorecard**, not new theory.

---

## 1. The headline

- **13.5%** of the low-emissions deployment needed by 2050 is done (end-2024), up ~3pp in two years.
- **17.2%** is where we would be at "**cruising speed**".
- → **The transition is running at roughly half the required pace.**

*"Cruising speed" is the one new concept: a constant required annual build rate consistent with
holding warming well below 2 °C. A speedometer, not a forecast.*

**3 of 7 domains accelerating** — power, mobility, critical minerals.
**4 stuck** — buildings, heavy industry, hydrogen, carbon capture.

---

## 2. The scorecard — annual additions (Exhibit 3)

| **Mobility** (EV sales) | 10 M | **17 M** | 58 M |

| **Buildings** (heat pump sales) | 111 GW | **108 GW** | 286 GW |

## 3. China is doing most of it

- ~**2/3** of global solar+wind and EV additions since 2022; ~**3/4** in H1 2025
- **65%** of global EV sales in H1 2025
- China is at **19%** of its own EV deployment need vs **3%** everywhere else
- ~**90%** of the world's electric trucks; ~**70%** of public charging points;
  ~**80%** of battery recycling capacity
- Yet fossil fuels are still ~87% of China's energy supply and >60% of its generation

**Implication:** global averages now largely describe China. Any non-China analysis — including
ours for India — must be done separately or it will mislead.

---

## 4. India shows up well — the Phase 2 anchor

> **India added more than 20 GW of solar and wind in H1 2025 — more than the United States did
> in the same period.** *(verify against CEA / MNRE monthly installed-capacity data)*

- Emerging economies ex-China installed ~30 GW in H1 2025, about the same as the entire EU —
  and India was roughly two-thirds of that
- **India's solar and wind deployment has grown almost 80% since 2022**
- Emerging-market EV share moved 3% → 5% → 7%
- India allocated **$240 million** to EV charging infrastructure in 2024, while new EV policies
  capped investments eligible for tariff relief
- India's energy-efficiency improvement in 2024: **1.5%** (China 2%, US 1.1%, EU 0.6%;
  ~4% is what net-zero scenarios need)
- New nuclear reactors expected online end-2025 in China, **India** and South Korea

---

## 5. Where the West slowed

- **US:** 40 GW added in 2024 (+60% vs 2022) but **flat in H1 2025**; EV sales plateaued.
  The **One Big Beautiful Bill (OBBB) Act** phases out incentives for wind, solar, low-emissions
  hydrogen and EVs, while *expanding* support for critical minerals, some low-emissions fuels and
  some carbon-use cases (including enhanced oil recovery). Analysts: US EV share by 2030 ~**25%**,
  about half prior projections; solar+wind build to 2030 ~**30% lower** than under old policy.
  $3.7 bn of DOE CCUS grants cancelled in 2025.
- **EU:** 70 GW in 2024 (+40%) but no year-on-year growth in 2025. EV sales +20% in H1 2025.
  Heat pump sales **−21%**, led by France and Germany. Clean Industrial Deal launched, but several
  member states cut support for solar, EVs and heat pumps.
- **China policy:** unified electricity market for renewables under the 2024 Energy Law; national
  ETS extended to cement, steel and aluminium.
- **Tariffs:** McKinsey estimates ~6% slower US/EU solar+wind by 2035 and 2–3% higher system costs —
  but possibly *faster* deployment elsewhere if China redirects cheap supply.
- **AI / data centres:** globally only ~5% of electricity demand (less than air conditioning), but
  up to **15% of US demand by 2030**. Mixed effect — strains grids, but funds solar-plus-storage and
  clean firm power, and may become a new source of demand-side flexibility.

## 6. The 25 challenges, re-scored

**Level 1 & 2 (13 challenges): 6 mostly positive, 6 neutral, 1 mostly negative.**
The easier stuff is broadly working.

**The single negative is the grid (challenge 5) — the most important finding in the report:**
- Global grid spend rose to **$390 bn** in 2024 (+~20% vs 2022) — still below requirement
- Transformer and cable costs **up ~35%**; lead times **doubled to 2–3 years**
- Solar and wind **awaiting grid connection: 1.50 TW (2023) → 1.65 TW (2024)** — about
  **six times Germany's entire generation capacity**, built and idle
- Skilled-labour shortage is the next constraint behind it

**Level 3, the "demanding dozen": 5 mostly negative, 5 neutral, 2 mostly positive.** Still stuck.
- Negative: aviation & shipping fuels (11), steel (12), ammonia (15), hydrogen (20 and 21)
- Positive within Level 3: electric trucks (9); at Level 1, BEV breakeven (7) and BEV range (8)

---

## 7. Four genuine bright spots

1. **Batteries.** LFP now **50%** of EV batteries (+10pp since 2022) and ~**30% less
   carbon-intensive** than NMC — this alone improves the Module 1C breakeven maths. Sodium-ion
   +10% density (175 Wh/kg); LMFP +20% over LFP; first commercial solid-state at 260 Wh/kg.
   Cars in China now charging **400–500 km in 5 minutes**. Storage costs fell ~20% in 2024;
   global battery storage investment **$55 bn**, ~3× 2022.
2. **Electric trucks.** Stock **more than tripled** to 400,000 (90% in China, still <1% of the 2050
   target). Ranges 500–600 km. Megawatt charging in 15–30 minutes. They are now taking use cases
   that were assumed to need hydrogen.
3. **Cement.** World's **first full-scale cement CCUS plant** came online in Norway; a plasma-heated
   electric kiln ran 54 hours uninterrupted in Sweden; oxyfuel kiln CCUS started in Germany.
   The hardest industrial problem has cracked open.
4. **Clean firm power.** 60+ reactors under construction (half in China); SMR construction started in
   Argentina, China, Russia and the US; geothermal **drilling times down as much as 80%**;
   25 countries now back tripling nuclear by 2050; a World Bank–IAEA partnership for emerging markets.

---

## 8. Four genuine setbacks

1. **Hydrogen.** **50+ projects cancelled** 2024–mid-2025. Investment reaching FID doubled to $75 bn —
   but that is only **10%** of announced investment. Of ~40,000 km of announced H₂ pipelines, only
   **2% reached FID**. Planned storage is **1/20th** of the 2035 requirement (10 TWh vs 230 TWh).
   The IEA revised electrolyser and hydrogen costs **upward**.
2. **Steel.** ~**600 Mt of global overcapacity** (~25% of capacity) crushed prices and killed the
   green-steel business case. H₂-DRI projects cancelled in Germany and the US. Molten oxide
   electrolysis demo launched in the US is the one bright note.
3. **Heat pumps.** Global sales −1% in 2024; EU **−21%**. Cold-climate units delivering full capacity
   at −15 °C are nearing commercialisation, but grid lead times now threaten future rollout.
4. **Direct air capture.** Global capacity **0.02 Mt**; pipeline ~12 Mt against 85 Mt needed by 2030.
   The largest facility revised its cost projections **worse**.

Also stalled: SAF at **0.3%** of jet fuel; only ~2% of EU shipping e-fuel projects funded; IMO
emission-intensity rules adjourned a year.

---

## 9. The one line to remember

> For years, hydrogen was assumed to be the answer for trucks and cement. In two years,
> **denser batteries and electric/plasma kilns overtook it.** There is rarely a single route
> forward — and the winning route can change fast.

The report's advice: advantage comes from **anticipating the next constraint and positioning early
to remove it.** For Level 1 and 2 challenges, execution is now the differentiator, not technology
choice. Level 3 still needs innovation.

---

## Phase 2 / Phase 3 hooks

- **Lead the India deck with:** >20 GW solar+wind in H1 2025, beating the US; ~80% growth since 2022.
- **The grid is the world's #1 bottleneck** — 1.65 TW in queues, transformers +35%, 2–3 year lead
  times. This is the strongest possible set-up for the Energy Group transmission argument. Build the Indian
  equivalent: ISTS connectivity queue, CEA transmission plan, domestic transformer/conductor lead times.
- **Cement is now a live decarbonisation market, not a research topic.** Direct read-across to
  Ambuja + ACC.
- **Steel overcapacity (600 Mt)** is why green steel stalled globally — check whether India's
  demand growth insulates it, unlike Europe.
- **Be sceptical on hydrogen in the India deck.** Global 2024–25 evidence is clearly negative.
  Frame NGHM honestly against the cancellations, and favour refineries / fertiliser / ammonia
  export over mobility.
- **Critical minerals are the one domain running above pace globally** — supply +20%, lithium +65%,
  cobalt +45%, but copper only +5%. This weakens the "global shortage" framing while leaving India's
  *import dependence* framing fully intact.
- **Data centres** are a two-sided story worth using: Energy Group ConneX is a demand-side risk *and* an
  anchor offtaker for firm clean power.

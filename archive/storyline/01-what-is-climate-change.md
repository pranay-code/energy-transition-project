# Q1 — What is climate — is it the same thing as weather? What about this "climate change"?

No, they are not the same. Weather is the state of the atmosphere right now — hot, wet, windy, calm —
and it can change by the hour. Climate is the summary of that weather, averaged over many years: what
a place is normally like. Today's downpour in Mumbai is weather; the fact that Mumbai's rain reliably
comes in June and not in January is its climate. Climate change means that long-run "normal" itself
starts to shift — for example, summers that grow hotter decade after decade, or a monsoon that slowly
starts arriving later than it used to.

What about the rate? The shift is real, but what alarms scientists is how fast it is happening. The
Earth's climate has changed before, over ice ages that came and went — yet always at a crawl. Coming
out of the last ice age, the planet warmed by about 1 °C roughly every 1,700 years. This time we have
warmed by about 1 °C in just the last 40 or so years, since around 1980. This is what we call rapid
climate change.

*Sources: IPCC AR6 WG1 (palaeoclimate warming rates, deglacial period); HadCRUT5 and NOAA GML (recent global temperature), 2025.*

# Q9 — So what exactly has India signed up for under the Paris Agreement?

Under Paris, every country writes its own plan. India's plan has three numbers in it.

- **Net zero by 2070.** Not 2050, like most rich countries. India asked for twenty extra years, and
  the ask was fair. Of all the carbon humans have ever put into the air, India put in just 3 to 4
  percent — the rich countries put in most of it. And crores of Indians are still waiting for
  steady power at home.
- **45 percent less carbon for the same amount of work, by 2030, compared with 2005.** Add up
  everything India makes and does in a year — the steel, the cement, the cloth, the bus rides, the
  phone calls. That is the country's work. Today, every bit of that work carries some carbon. By
  2030 it must carry nearly half as much. Like a scooter covering the same road on far less petrol.
  India will keep growing and doing more work — it has only promised that each bit of it gets much
  cleaner.
- **Half of all power capacity from clean sources by 2030 — about 500 gigawatts.** Sun, wind, water
  and nuclear. One gigawatt can light up a small city. India actually passed that half mark early
  — by the middle of 2026, clean sources made up 54 percent of its power capacity. The full 500
  gigawatts is a longer walk: about 297 built so far.

The 2030 date is the one that bites, because it is less than five years away. And a bigger clock is
ticking behind it — the world can let out only a few hundred billion more tonnes of carbon before
1.5 °C slips away, roughly ten years at today's speed. So India's targets are clear, countable and
close. But you cannot cut what you cannot find. Where is all that carbon actually coming from?

*Sources: India's updated NDC, UNFCCC, 2022 (the 45 percent target and the 50 percent clean-capacity share); COP26 "Panchamrit" pledge, Glasgow, 2021, later carried into India's long-term strategy filed under Paris (net zero 2070, 500 GW); Central Electricity Authority, installed capacity as on 30 June 2026 (297,369 MW non-fossil of 548,858 MW total — 54.18 percent); IPCC AR6 WG1, 2023 (carbon budget remaining); Global Carbon Project, 2023 (India's share of past emissions).*

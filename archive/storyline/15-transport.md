# Q15 — Transport: emission sources, solutions, challenges, and progress against targets?

Transport looks like the easy one. It is about a tenth of India's carbon — less than power, less
than industry, less than farming. And unlike a steel furnace or a paddy field, everybody already
agrees on the answer: run it on electricity instead of petrol.

Then you look at what India actually burns to move things about. Two-thirds of it is diesel. And
diesel is not what scooters and cars drink.

The vehicles that matter here are not the ones in the news.

---

## 1. Where the carbon comes from

**Transport is 9.2 percent of everything India emits** — the smallest of the five sectors. In 2020
that came to **297 million tonnes** of greenhouse gas. Almost all of it comes off the road.

| Mode | What that means | Tonnes a year | Of sector | Of India |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Road** | Trucks, buses, cars, scooters | 279 Mt | 94% | **8.6%** |
| **Aeroplanes** | Flights inside India | 12 Mt | 4% | **0.4%** |
| **Railways** | Only the diesel engines | 3 Mt | 1% | **0.1%** |
| **Ships and boats** | Coastal and river craft | 3 Mt | 1% | **0.1%** |

Road transport alone is 8.6 percent of India's carbon — bigger than every other form of movement
put together, several times over.

So "transport" really means "road". But that still hides the important part. The government also
publishes how much of each fuel the road burned. Turn those fuel numbers into carbon and a different
picture appears.

| Fuel burned on the road | Who burns it | Tonnes a year | Share of road |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diesel** | Trucks, buses, delivery vans | 184 Mt | 66% |
| **Petrol** | Scooters, motorcycles, cars | 80 Mt | 29% |
| **CNG and LPG** | Gas autos and city buses | 15 Mt | 5% |

Diesel is the fuel of work. Petrol is the fuel of getting about. So the sector that looks like an
electric-scooter story is, by weight of carbon, a lorry story.

How lopsided? Trucks are about **three percent of the vehicles on Indian roads and roughly a third
of the carbon.** Estimates run from a third to 44 percent, so take the middle — about 100 million
tonnes. Either way the shape is not in doubt.

Now the line that changes everything. Railways show up above as one percent. That is only their
diesel. India's electric trains used **31.8 billion units of electricity in 2023-24**, and that gets
counted under power stations, not under transport. At India's grid, that electricity carries about
**23 million tonnes of carbon** — eight times the railway's visible figure. The railway did not make
its carbon vanish. It moved it to a different page.

Hold that thought. It is the whole question.

---

## 2. The solutions, ranked

The ranking is simply the size of the fuel pool each one attacks, and every tonne of the 297 sits in
exactly one row below. Nothing is counted twice.

**2.1 Take the diesel out of moving goods — 184 million tonnes.** Two roads to the same tonnes. Put
the truck on a battery, or put the load on a train. India already has the train: 99.6 percent of its
main railway runs on wires.

**2.2 Take the petrol out of the two-wheeler and the car — 80 million tonnes.** Again two roads.
Change the vehicle, or change the fuel it drinks. India is doing both.

**2.3 Make the gas at home instead of buying it — 15 million tonnes.** CNG autos and buses already
burn something cleaner than diesel. Making that gas from crop waste and dung, instead of buying it
off a ship, removes most of what is left.

**2.4 Find a liquid an aeroplane can burn — 12 million tonnes.** Small today, and growing fastest.

**2.5 Finish the railway and the boats — 6 million tonnes.** The smallest job, and the nearest to
done.

---

## 3. The challenge behind each

Farming had no place in the world's standard list of physical challenges. Transport does — five of
the twenty-five named challenges sit here. So these are the recognised ones, applied to India. Each
solution is short of exactly one thing.

**3.1 Moving goods is short of room.** A truck doing long trips needs 500 to 700 units of electricity
on board for a normal day. At today's battery weight that is about **three tonnes** — three tonnes of
paying load it can no longer carry. Then it needs hours plugged into a connection bigger than most
small factories have.

The train has no weight problem at all. Its problem is that India barely uses it. About **71 percent
of goods still travel by road**. So there are two shortages, not one: no room on the truck for the
battery, and no habit of putting the load on the train.

**3.2 The small vehicle is short of a clean plug.** The scooter itself is solved — small battery,
efficient motor, the machine works. What it plugs into is the problem. India's grid puts out about
**710 grams of carbon for every unit of electricity**. An electric vehicle is not a clean product. It
is a claim on whatever the grid is burning at the moment you charge it.

A battery costs carbon to build before the wheels turn, so an electric vehicle starts life owing
carbon and pays it back kilometre by kilometre. Worked out on India's real grid:

| What we are comparing | Electric scooter | Electric car |
|---|---|---|
| Battery carried on board | 3 units | 40 units |
| Carbon to build that battery | 180 kg | 2,400 kg |
| Petrol version, per km | 46 g | 154 g |
| Electric version today, per km | 23 g | 117 g |
| Carbon saved per km | 23 g | 37 g |
| Distance to pay off the battery | **7,800 km** | **65,000 km** |
| How long that takes in India | about **1 year** | about **7 years** |

**The scooter's answer is solid.** Whatever petrol scooter you compare it to, the electric one uses
roughly half the carbon per kilometre. A rider covering 8,000 to 10,000 km a year is ahead inside the
first year, on today's dirty grid, with no help from anybody.

**The car's answer is fragile.** Hold it against a really efficient small petrol car doing 20
kilometres to the litre and, on today's grid, it **never breaks even at all**. Its battery debt is
thirteen times bigger, so the verdict flips depending on which petrol car you pick. Clean the grid to
500 grams and the scooter pays off in 6,000 km, the car in 34,000 km. Both improve only if the power
sector improves.

**3.3 Gas is short of gas.** India buys about half its natural gas from abroad, so every CNG bus is
partly an import. The homegrown answer means collecting crop waste and dung from crores of scattered
places — the same shortage farming already ran into.

**3.4 The aeroplane is short of a molecule.** A kilogram of jet fuel holds roughly fifty times the
energy of a kilogram of battery, and a plane must lift its own fuel. Even allowing for the fact that
a jet engine wastes far more of its fuel than a motor wastes its charge, the gap is still about
twenty to one. You cannot plug in a plane. The only route is a liquid made from plants or waste, and
the constraint is finding enough waste that isn't already spoken for.

**3.5 The ship is short of numbers.** Railways are 99.6 percent electrified; what is left is shunting
yards and 269 kilometres of track. Ships are the opposite — a clean marine engine burning ammonia or
methanol exists, but in ones and twos, not in a catalogue you can order from.

---

## 4. Where it actually stands

### 4.1 Built and working

- **The railway is done.** As on 31 May 2026, **70,002 of 70,271 route kilometres** of broad gauge
  are electrified — **99.6 percent**, with 269 km left across five states. More electrified than the
  European Union, Britain or America.
- **E20 arrived five years early.** Petrol sold anywhere in India is now 20 percent ethanol, up from
  under 1.5 percent in 2013-14. The target date was 2030.
- **EV sales crossed 25 lakh.** 25,50,865 in FY2025-26 — **8.64 percent of every vehicle sold in
  India**. Two- and three-wheelers were 93 percent of them.
- **The auto is the star.** **33.7 percent** of new passenger three-wheelers are electric, up from
  20.8 percent two years earlier. One in three.

### 4.2 Started, not finished

- **Charging is the bottleneck, and it keeps repeating.** Under the old FAME-II scheme, 2,877
  charging stations were approved, 1,822 were awarded, and **83 were built.** Under the new PM
  E-DRIVE scheme, ₹688.84 crore and 6,562 stations have been sanctioned — and in a written answer to
  Parliament in August 2026 the government confirmed that **not one has been installed and not one
  rupee has been spent.**
- **The truck has not started.** 14,803 electric goods vehicles sold in FY2025-26 — **1.6 percent** of
  new ones, against 184 million tonnes of diesel carbon.
- **The car and the scooter are slow.** Electric cars are 4.6 percent of new car sales. Electric
  two-wheelers are 6.6 percent, up just **0.4 of a point in a year** — in the one segment where the
  carbon maths already works.
- **Aeroplanes are at zero.** India has agreed to blend 1 percent cleaner jet fuel by 2027, 2 percent
  by 2028 and 5 percent by 2030, starting with international flights. Nothing is in force today.

### 4.3 Proof the unfinished part can finish

Look again at the three-wheeler. Two years ago one in five new passenger autos was electric. Now it
is one in three. It happened with far less noise and far less money than the scooter push, for one
reason: **the sums work for the driver.** An auto covering 100 to 150 kilometres a day pays back the
higher price in months. Nobody had to be persuaded — the arithmetic persuaded them. A truck runs even
more kilometres than an auto. What it does not have is a charger.

---

## 5. Progress against 2030 and 2070

First, what has actually been saved. Here is the government's own list, with what each measure
reached into.

| Measure | What it changed | Carbon cut | Period |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ethanol in petrol** | The fuel, at the refinery | 31.8 Mt | 2014 to 2022 |
| **Fuel-economy rules** | The vehicle, at the factory | 4.4 Mt | 2019 to 2023 |
| **Metro rail** | The trip, in big cities | 0.9 Mt | 2002 to 2015 |
| **EV purchase subsidy** | The buyer's own choice | 0.4 Mt | 2018 to 2022 |
| **Bus corridors** | The trip, in 11 cities | 0.04 Mt | 2006 to 2015 |
| **All five together** | — | **37.5 Mt** | mixed |

The periods overlap, so the total is rough. These are also tonnes *avoided* — carbon that would have
been released otherwise — not a drop in what India records. But read the second column down the page
and the answer is sitting there. **Ethanol did 85 percent of it.** Every rupee of EV subsidy in the
country bought 0.4 million tonnes; pouring alcohol into petrol at the refinery bought 31.8 million —
about **seventy-five times more**. Rules binding the manufacturer bought ten times more than the
subsidy did. The lever that moved at national speed was fitted inside a pipe, before the customer,
where nobody had to choose anything.

Now the same five solutions re-sorted, not by size but by how far along each is. The order turns
almost upside down.

| Solution | Carbon it could remove | Where it is now | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.5 Railway and boats | 6 Mt a year | 99.6% electrified | Effectively done |
| 2.3 Gas and blending | 15 Mt a year | E20 met, 5 years early | Ahead of target |
| 2.2 Petrol vehicles | 80 Mt a year | autos 34%, scooters 7%, cars 5% | Only the auto moving |
| 2.4 Aeroplanes | 12 Mt a year | no blending in force | Not begun |
| 2.1 Diesel for goods | 184 Mt a year | electric trucks 1.6% | Barely begun |

**The railway is the success and the warning together.** India finished a job most countries have
not — but the 23 million tonnes those trains emit did not disappear. It walked over to the power
sector. Electrifying is not the end of the work. It is the handover.

**Blending beat everything, and it has a ceiling.** Nine years of ethanol cut 31.8 million tonnes,
against a petrol pool of 80 million every year. Going past 20 percent runs into engines and into
farmland.

**The petrol pool is splitting in two.** The auto races because the money works for the person
paying. The scooter crawls at 6.6 percent even though its carbon maths is the best in the sector.
India is not electrifying evenly — it is electrifying wherever the vehicle earns its living.

**And the biggest prize is the emptiest box.** 184 million tonnes of diesel, and 1.6 percent of new
goods vehicles electric. Nothing else here comes close in size, and nothing else has moved less.

### 5.1 The number nobody set a target for

India's pledge to the world sets no transport target. No EV target, no goods target, no aeroplane
target, no date. The only number transport is measured against is the whole-economy one: **45 percent
less carbon per unit of output by 2030, against 2005.** The nearest thing to a sector goal — 30
percent of vehicles electric by 2030 — came from a government think tank, was never written into the
pledge, and no ministry answers for it. For 2070 there is nothing at all.

So the honest score for transport against a transport target is the same as farming's: **there isn't
one.**

But the number that decides this sector is not in the transport chapter anyway. It is **710** — grams
of carbon in one unit of India's electricity. Every solution above ends by handing its carbon to the
grid. The scooter hands it over. The truck will. The railway already did, all 23 million tonnes of
it. Clean the grid and every one of them gets cleaner together, with no extra vehicle sold. Leave it
as it is, and India will have spent a fortune moving carbon from a tailpipe to a chimney.

There is one more twist, and it is unkind. People plug in the scooter after getting home from work.
India's demand peaks at **270,832 megawatts at 22:36** — after dark, when there is no sun left. The
charging arrives at the exact hour the grid has least to give.

And on a hot evening in May, at that very same hour, tens of crores of Indians reach for a different
switch.

*Sources: India's Fourth Biennial Update Report to the UNFCCC (MoEFCC) for the 2020 figures, the mode
and fuel splits, and the scheme savings table. Ministry of Railways, 31 May 2026, for 70,002 of
70,271 route km. Press Information Bureau for ethanol. Vahan dashboard via EVreporter for FY2025-26
sales. Lok Sabha written answer, Ministry of Heavy Industries, August 2026, for the 6,562 sanctioned
and zero installed chargers. Central Electricity Authority for 710 g/kWh and the 22:36 peak. Truck
share range from NITI Aayog, ICCT and WRI India. Ministry of Civil Aviation jet-fuel targets as
reported in May 2026.* ***Ours, not sourced:*** *the diesel-petrol-gas split of road carbon, the
23 Mt of railway electricity, and the whole table in section 3.2, which assumes 60 kg of carbon per
unit of battery built (published estimates run 22 to 75), 10 percent charging loss, 30 Wh per km for
a scooter and 150 Wh per km for a car. India's gas import share and the 71 percent road share of
goods are from general reporting, not a primary file. The physical-challenge framework is McKinsey
Global Institute's.*

# Working notes — not part of the answer

## Structure

Same locked template as Q12, Q13 and Q14. Five beats with the titles word for word. Beats 2 and 3
item-locked, `section.item` numbering. Beat 4 as the three-part ladder. Beat 5 closes with "the
number nobody set a target for". One italic source line only, at the very bottom — an earlier draft
had two and that breaks rule 4.2.

**The spine is: transport's answer is written on the power question's page.** Everything in this
sector ends by handing its carbon to the grid. The railway already finished the handover and its
carbon is now 23 Mt sitting in the electricity line.

**The second spine, carried over from Q14: upstream beats downstream.** Ethanol at the refinery cut
31.8 Mt. Fuel-economy rules at the factory cut 4.4 Mt. The consumer subsidy cut 0.4 Mt. Same shape as
neem-coated urea doing 79% of farming's total. Two sectors, same finding, arrived at independently.
This is now a load-bearing pattern for Q17.

## How the beat-1 fuel split was built

BUR-4 Table 2.9 gives road fuel consumption in TJ for 2020: petrol 11,16,171 · diesel 24,09,405 ·
CNG 2,48,319 · LPG 6,286 · LDO 69. Applied IPCC 2006 default factors (petrol 69.3, diesel 74.1, gas
56.1, LPG 63.1 tCO₂/TJ):

- Petrol 77.4 Mt · Diesel 178.5 Mt · CNG 13.9 Mt · LPG 0.4 Mt → **270.2 Mt of CO₂**
- BUR-4's own road total (94% of 297,371 Gg) is **279.5 Mt of CO₂e**, which also carries CH₄ and N₂O

Our CO₂-only figure is 97% of theirs — good agreement. The shares (diesel 66.1%, petrol 28.6%, gas
5.3%) were then applied to their 279.5 Mt to give 184 / 80 / 15. Stated in the source line as ours.

**Caution on 2020.** It is a COVID year. Petrol fell and diesel *rose* 11% — BUR-4 attributes part of
this to "changes in methodology and allocation of consumption". On 2019 numbers the split is diesel
62.6% / petrol 34.0%, so "about two-thirds diesel" is safe either way and the exact 66% is not.

## The truck share — sources disagree, and the answer says so

| Source | Claim |
|---|---|
| ICCT, Sept 2025 | Medium and heavy trucks = **44%** of transport emissions, 3% of vehicles |
| NITI Aayog (ZET report) | Trucks = **one-third** of transport CO₂ |
| WRI India, 2023 | Goods vehicles = **34%** of road transport emissions, 28.25% of diesel |
| LBL / Karali & Abhyankar | Trucks + buses = **54%** of road diesel → about 100 Mt |

Three of four cluster at a third. Our own cross-check: 66% of road carbon is diesel, and trucks and
buses take 54% of road diesel → 36% of road carbon. The answer takes the middle (~100 Mt, a third)
and states the range openly rather than picking a favourite.

## The EV breakeven table — every assumption

All ours. Nothing in section 3.2 is a sourced figure.

| Input | Value used | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Grid intensity | 710 gCO₂/kWh | CEA, FY2024-25 (the corrected figure) |
| Battery manufacturing | 60 kg CO₂/kWh | Mid-to-high of published 22–75 range |
| Charging loss | 10% | Standard allowance |
| Scooter battery | 3 kWh | Typical Indian high-speed e-2W |
| Scooter energy use | 30 Wh/km | Typical; 33 Wh/km after charging loss |
| Petrol scooter | 50 km/l, 2.31 kg CO₂/l → 46 g/km | Tailpipe only, no upstream — deliberately generous to the petrol side |
| Car battery | 40 kWh | Typical Indian e-4W |
| Car energy use | 150 Wh/km | Real-world; 165 Wh/km after loss |
| Petrol car | 15 km/l → 154 g/km | Average, not best |

**Why MGI's India conclusion does not transfer.** MGI ran the analysis on cars only, at 841 g/kWh,
and found breakeven beyond 250,000 km. At the correct 710 g/kWh against an *average* petrol car the
car breaks even at 65,000 km; against an efficient 20 km/l car it still never breaks even. So MGI's
finding survives in spirit for the car and collapses completely for the scooter, whose battery debt
is 13× smaller. That is the whole point of the section — the pessimism was a car finding, and India
is not electrifying cars.

**Sensitivity, stated in the answer:** at 500 g/kWh the scooter pays off in 6,100 km and the car in
33,600 km.

## The railway's hidden 23 Mt

BUR-4 says plainly that railway electricity is excluded from transport "to avoid double counting" and
is reported under 1A1a, electricity generation. Railways used 31.81 TWh in FY2023-24 (Ministry of
Railways; 19.02 TWh in FY2017-18). At 710 g/kWh that is 22.6 Mt. Note the year mismatch — inventory
is 2020, electricity figure is FY2023-24. Called out as ours in the source line.

## Where this points for the payoff

Three things Q17 should pick up, and none of them is a vehicle:

1. **Charging is an infrastructure business with a proven delivery failure.** 83 built out of 2,877
   under one scheme; 0 out of 6,562 under the next. The bottleneck is not demand and not technology.
   It is somebody actually building and connecting them.
2. **The upstream lever wins twice in a row.** Refinery blending in transport, factory-gate urea in
   farming. Whoever owns the pipe, the plant or the port moves national numbers; whoever sells to the
   consumer does not.
3. **Every tonne here lands on the grid at the evening peak.** That is the same 22:36 hour Q12
   identified and the same hour Q16 is about to make worse. Storage and transmission at that hour is
   the asset that pays off across three sectors at once.

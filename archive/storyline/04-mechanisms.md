# Q4 — So how exactly are we doing it? What are all the ways?

The biggest way, by far, is burning. We set coal, oil and gas on fire to make electricity, to run
our cars, buses, planes and ships, and to power factories. Every time we do, greenhouse gases go up
into the air. In fact, this one act of burning causes about three out of every four tonnes of those
gases. So burning fuel is the main villain here.

But is that everything? Not quite. When we cut down forests, the carbon stored in the trees escapes —
and we lose the very plants that were cleaning the air for us. Farming adds more: cattle burp out a
gas called methane, flooded rice fields give off even more, and fertiliser releases another strong
gas. Even making cement lets out carbon from the rock it is baked from. So most of it is burning, but
our forests, farms and factories all add to the pile. Now the real question — how do we stop it?

*Sources: Global Carbon Budget, 2024; IPCC AR6 WG3; Climate Watch / WRI (sector shares).*

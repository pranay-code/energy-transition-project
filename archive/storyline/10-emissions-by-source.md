# Q10 — Fine, but where is all this carbon actually coming from?

Take 2023, the latest year with a full count. In the world overall, making electricity is the
single biggest source at 33 percent. Transport comes next at 19 percent, then industry at 18,
farming at 12, and buildings at 6. That is the world's pie. India's is cut quite differently.

- **Power — 37 percent.** India's biggest slice by far. The reason is coal: roughly seven out of
  every ten units of Indian electricity are made by burning it, against three in ten worldwide.
- **Industry — 22 percent.** Cement, steel, bricks, fertiliser, chemicals. A country still being
  built needs enormous amounts of all five.
- **Farming — 20 percent.** Well above the world's 12 percent. India has the largest cattle herd on
  Earth, and paddy fields sitting under water give off methane for months.
- **Transport — 9 percent.** Barely half the world's 19 percent. Most Indian families still do not
  own a car, and most journeys happen on two wheels, a bus or a train.
- **Buildings, waste and gas leaks — 12 percent.** Everything left over.

So India's carbon is not spread evenly. One slice stands well above the rest — power at 37 percent,
against 22 for the next biggest. And nothing else can make up for it. Even if every car, bus, truck
and train in India ran clean from tomorrow, that would remove only 9 percent. Power alone decides
whether India meets its 2030 promise or misses it. Which raises the obvious question: what actually
replaces each of these?

*Sources: Climate Watch / World Resources Institute sector data for 2023, via Our World in Data, retrieved 7 Aug 2026 (all shares; totals are 4.14 billion tonnes of greenhouse gases for India and 51.5 billion for the world, counting methane and other gases alongside carbon dioxide, and leaving out land use, which is a net absorber in India); Ember electricity data for 2025 (coal's share of generation — India 70.8 percent, world 33.0 percent). Shares rounded to the nearest whole number.*

**Source:** Climate Watch / WRI via Our World in Data, retrieved 7 Aug 2026. Grouped from ten
reported categories into six: *Industry* = manufacturing and construction + industrial processes;
*Transport* = transport + aviation and shipping; *Waste, gas leaks and the rest* = fugitive
emissions + waste + other fuel combustion.

**Note on the year.** Climate Watch's sector split stops at 2023 — confirmed against their own API
on 7 Aug 2026, so no newer breakdown exists. India's *total* CO₂ for 2024 is available and is 3.19
billion tonnes (Global Carbon Budget), but it carries no sector detail.

**Held back for the core question.** Of only the carbon India makes by *burning fuel* — cattle and
paddy fields set aside — power accounts for 53 percent, against 46 percent worldwide. It is a
sharper number than the 37 percent above, but the two measure different things, and putting both in
one runway answer confuses the reader. Use it where there is room to explain the difference.

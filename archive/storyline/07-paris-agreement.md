# Q7 — What is the Paris Agreement? Is it just a simple net-zero target, or something more objective — does it get into the details of what to do?

At the top, Paris is very objective. Nearly every country signed up to one measurable goal: hold the
Earth's warming well below 2 °C, and try to keep it to 1.5 °C. And that warming is measured against
how cool the planet was before the industrial age — roughly the late 1800s, before we started burning
fuel in a big way. To reach that goal, the world agreed to bring emissions down to net zero by around
the middle of this century — about 2050. So it isn't a vague "let's go green"; it's a hard number and
a clear deadline the whole world can be measured against.

But does it get into the finer details? Not really — and that's by design. Paris sets the
destination, not the exact route. It fixes one firm, shared goal, and then trusts each country to find
its own way there. Which brings us to the real question — who is doing most of the emitting, and where
does India stand?

*Sources: UNFCCC Paris Agreement text; IPCC SR1.5 (net zero by ~2050 for 1.5 °C).*

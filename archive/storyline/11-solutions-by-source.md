# Q11 — Okay, we know the sources and the major ones among them — how are we going to reduce them and meet our targets?

Here is the good news: every one of those sectors has a known solution. Not a dream, not a
laboratory idea — a real solution that some country is already using today. And here is the catch:
every solution comes with a real challenge attached to it. Take them one at a time, biggest first.

- **Power, 37 percent.** *Solution:* solar and wind, which India is already building faster than
  almost anybody. *Challenge:* the sun sets at six, but India switches on its fans and air
  conditioners at eight.
- **Industry, 22 percent.** *Solution:* run furnaces on electricity, and make steel with hydrogen
  in place of coal. *Challenge:* these furnaces need extreme heat. And cement gives off carbon from
  the stone itself, not only from the fire — so a clean fire does not save you.
- **Farming, 20 percent.** *Solution:* better feed for cattle, and growing rice without keeping the
  field flooded all season. *Challenge:* there is no one factory to fix here. There are crores of
  small farmers, each deciding for themselves.
- **Buildings, waste and the rest, 12 percent.** *Solution:* efficient air conditioners, cleaner
  cooking fuel, better waste handling. *Challenge:* as India grows richer and hotter, the demand for
  cooling is climbing faster than the machines are improving.
- **Transport, 9 percent.** *Solution:* electric two- and three-wheelers, where India already leads
  the world. *Challenge:* an electric vehicle is only as clean as the electricity it drinks — and
  India's electricity is still nearly three-quarters fossil.

Now look at that list again. Every sector has a solution, and not one of them is easy. But notice
something else — the transport solution needs clean electricity, and so does the industry solution.
Almost every road leads back to the same place. So let us start where the carbon is thickest, and
where everything else is waiting: electricity.

*Sources: IEA World Energy Outlook and India Energy Outlook; McKinsey Global Institute, "The hard stuff", 2024–25 (the solutions and their physical limits); NITI Aayog (India-specific pathways); Ember, 2025 (India's electricity still 73.4 percent fossil). Sector shares are the 2023 figures.*

## Working notes — not for the reader

- Sector shares are 2023 (Climate Watch / WRI). The three-quarters-fossil electricity figure is
  2025 (Ember). Both flagged in the source line.
- Rows are ordered largest first everywhere in this answer — bullets and Exhibit A both — so the
  reader never has to ask why something sits where it does.
- Bar scale: 1 block = 2 percentage points, rounded to the nearest block.
- The solutions listed are the mature ones only. Deliberately left out: CCUS, direct air capture and
  hydrogen for anything other than steel — all under 1 percent deployed, and naming them here would
  make the picture look fuller than it is.
- No difficulty is explained in this answer beyond one line per sector. The *why* belongs to the
  sector questions that follow.

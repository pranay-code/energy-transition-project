# Q16 — Buildings, cooling, waste and gas leaks: emission sources, solutions, challenges, and progress against targets?

That switch is a fan, a cooler or an air conditioner. But leave the electricity aside — that carbon
was counted at the power station. What is left is what a building burns inside itself, what a city
leaves behind, and what escapes from the ground.

**Buildings, cooling, waste and gas leaks are 12.1 percent of everything India emits.** This is the
"everything left over" slice — the only one of the five that is not a single industry.

It is also the one where the fastest-growing source has no number at all.

---

## 1. Where the carbon comes from

| Where it comes from | What that means | Tonnes | Of sector | Of India |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Homes** | Cooking and heating fuel | 148.0 Mt | 41.4% | **5.0%** |
| **Shops, offices, hospitals** | Coal, diesel, gas on site | 92.6 Mt | 25.9% | **3.1%** |
| **Household sewage** | Rotting without air | 34.1 Mt | 9.5% | **1.2%** |
| **Gas leaks** | Escaping from coal mines and pipes | 26.9 Mt | 7.5% | **0.9%** |
| **Factory waste water** | Rotting without air | 21.8 Mt | 6.1% | **0.7%** |
| **Rubbish dumps** | Rotting without air | 19.1 Mt | 5.3% | **0.6%** |
| **Wood and dung fires** | Non-carbon gases only | 14.8 Mt | 4.1% | **0.5%** |

Homes alone are **5 percent of India's carbon** — India's own count ranks it the fifth-biggest single
category in the whole country. Household sewage is nearly twice the rubbish dumps, which surprises
almost everybody.

Now the two things this table leaves out.

**The wood fires.** India burns enough wood, dung and crop waste to release about **798 million
tonnes of carbon dioxide a year**. That is more than a quarter of everything the country emits, if it
were counted. The books list it and then keep it out, because the rule says a plant that grows back
takes its carbon with it. Only the soot and the other gases — that 14.8 Mt — get added in. The rule
is the world's rule, not India's. But the smoke is real, and it is in somebody's kitchen.

**The gas inside the cooling machine.** Every air conditioner and fridge holds a gas that traps heat
hundreds to thousands of times harder than carbon dioxide, and some of it leaks out over the
machine's life. In India's national count, the line for that gas reads **"not applicable"** — it is
not estimated at all. The only such gas India does report, 21.6 Mt, is a by-product from one chemical
process. Not a single molecule of it comes from a cooling machine.

That matters because of how fast the machines are arriving. About **8 percent** of Indian homes owned
an air conditioner in 2017-18. The government's own projection puts it at **21 percent by 2027-28 and
40 percent by 2037-38**. A record **1.25 crore room air conditioners** were sold in 2024-25 alone.
Every one of them is a small sealed tank of that gas.

---

## 2. The solutions, ranked

Ranked by how much of the sector each one reaches.

**2.1 Cook on gas instead of wood and coal — 148.0 Mt, 5.0 percent of India.** The largest item by
far, and the one that also touches the 798 million tonnes sitting outside the count.

**2.2 Take the coal and diesel out of shops, offices and hospitals — 92.6 Mt, 3.1 percent.** Second
largest, and the least examined.

**2.3 Treat the sewage before it rots — 34.1 Mt, 1.2 percent.** The biggest thing in the waste column
that a city can actually fix.

**2.4 Plug the gas leaks — 26.9 Mt, 0.9 percent.** Methane escaping from coal mines and gas pipes.
Two-thirds of it is coal.

**2.5 Clean the water leaving factories — 21.8 Mt, 0.7 percent.** Sits in the waste column but is not
a city's job at all.

**2.6 Keep wet rubbish out of the dump — 19.1 Mt, 0.6 percent.** Food waste buried under dry waste
makes methane. Composted or turned into gas, it does not.

**2.7 Change the gas inside the cooling machine — size unknown.** Last on this list only because
nobody can yet say how big it is. On growth alone it belongs near the top.

---

## 3. The challenge behind each

The world's standard list of physical challenges has exactly two entries for buildings, and both are
about keeping warm through a northern winter — heat pumps, and winter peaks. India's problem is the
opposite season. So the seven below are ours, not theirs.

**3.1 The kitchen is short of money for the refill, not the connection.** India has **32.94 crore**
working cooking-gas connections, including **10.33 crore** given free under Ujjwala. Coverage went
from 62 percent in 2016 to over 100 percent by 2023. That job is finished. But an Ujjwala household
buys about **3.8 cylinders a year**, and a family cooking every meal on gas needs eight or nine. The
stove is there. The wood fire is still lit beside it. A connection is one decision. A refill is a
decision every six weeks, against a few hundred rupees.

**3.2 The shop runs on diesel because the wire fails.** A hospital, a hotel, a mall or a small
workshop keeps a diesel generator not to save money but because the power goes. That emission is not
really a buildings problem — it is a reliability problem somewhere else. Until the wire is trusted,
the generator stays.

**3.3 The sewer is short of pipes and plants.** India's towns and cities produce **72,368 million
litres of sewage a day** and can treat about **31,841 million** — and actually treat around a third.
The rest runs into drains and rivers and rots without air, which is exactly how methane is made.
India built ten crore toilets in five years. The pipe at the other end did not keep pace.

**3.4 The leak is short of a place to catch it.** Half of all this methane comes from **open-cast
coal mines**, where the gas simply rises off an exposed seam across square kilometres of open pit.
There is no chimney to put a lid on. An underground mine can be tapped and the gas piped away. A
giant hole in the ground cannot.

**3.5 The factory's water has no owner in this sector.** 21.8 million tonnes, and no municipal scheme
reaches it, because it never enters a municipal drain. The fix sits inside the same plants that make
the steel, the paper and the sugar.

**3.6 The dump is short of separation.** Wet kitchen waste only makes methane because it is buried
under dry waste where no air reaches it. Separate the two at the doorstep and most of the problem
goes away. That means a daily habit in crores of homes — the same shortage farming ran into.

**3.7 The refrigerant is short of a safe replacement.** The gases that would replace today's ones are
either flammable, or work badly in 45-degree heat, or both. And the choice locks in for the
fifteen-year life of the machine, so a wrong pick today is still leaking in 2040.

---

## 4. Where it actually stands

### 4.1 Built and working

- **Cooking gas reached everybody.** Coverage went from 62 percent in 2016 to over 100 percent by
  2023. Kerosene burnt in Indian homes fell **56 percent in three years**.
- **Rubbish collection and processing.** Scientific processing of town waste went from **18 percent
  in 2014 to 73 percent**. **98 percent** of urban wards now have door-to-door collection.

### 4.2 Started, not finished

- **The refill.** 3.8 cylinders a year, against the eight or nine a full-time gas kitchen needs.
- **Sewage.** Treatment capacity covers under half of what is produced, and only about a third is
  actually treated. The biggest unfinished job in the sector.
- **Rubbish into fuel.** Ten plants making power from waste, **401.79 megawatts** in all, and 89
  plants making gas — real, but small against the size of the dumps.
- **The refrigerant switch has not begun.** India has agreed a timetable. The first step is a freeze
  in **2028**, then cuts running out to 2047.
- **The gas leaks have nothing pointed at them.** No Indian scheme currently targets mine methane.

### 4.3 Proof the unfinished part can finish

Look at what the rubbish programme did. In 2014, 18 percent of India's town waste was processed
properly. Ten years later it is 73 percent, with door-to-door collection in 98 wards out of every
100 — and ten crore toilets built along the way. That is not a pilot. That is a physical system
rebuilt across every municipality in the country inside a decade.

The sewage plant is the same kind of job: civil works, pipes, municipal money, a national deadline.
The country that did the first one can do the second. It has simply not been asked to with the same
force.

## 5. Progress against 2030 and 2070

| Solution | Size | Where it is now | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.6 Wet rubbish | 19.1 Mt, 0.6% | processing 18% to 73% | Well along |
| 2.1 Cooking on gas | 148.0 Mt, 5.0% | coverage 100%, refills 3.8 | Connected, not converted |
| 2.3 Household sewage | 34.1 Mt, 1.2% | about a third treated | Half built |
| 2.7 The refrigerant | not measured | first step due 2028 | On paper |
| 2.2 Shops and offices | 92.6 Mt, 3.1% | no scheme aimed at it | Not started |
| 2.4 Gas leaks | 26.9 Mt, 0.9% | no scheme aimed at it | Not started |
| 2.5 Factory water | 21.8 Mt, 0.7% | no scheme aimed at it | Not started |

Read the last column against the second. **The smallest item on the list is the one nearly finished,
and the second-largest has nothing pointed at it at all.**

**Cooking is the near-miss.** India solved the hard part — reaching thirty-three crore kitchens with
a pipe, a cylinder and a stove. What remains is a running cost of a few hundred rupees a month. A
smaller problem, and a stubborner one, because it comes back every six weeks.

**Sewage is the quiet failure.** Thirty-four million tonnes from households alone, nearly twice the
rubbish dumps, and no scheme in the national list is built around it.

**And shops and offices are the box nobody has opened.** Ninety-two million tonnes — 3.1 percent of
India's entire carbon, more than the whole aviation and railway and shipping business put together —
mostly coal and diesel burnt on site, with no programme attached. Part of the answer is not even in
this sector: give a hospital a wire it can trust and the generator switches off by itself.

### 5.1 The one dated promise, and what it is about

India's pledge to the world sets no buildings target, no cooking target and no waste target. As with
farming and transport, this sector is measured only against the whole-economy number: 45 percent less
carbon per unit of output by 2030, against 2005.

There is one exception, and it is a strange one. India has signed a dated, binding timetable to phase
down the refrigerant gas — a freeze in 2028, then step-by-step cuts to 2047. **The only firm promise
in this sector is about the one thing nobody measures.**

So the picture at the end of five sectors is oddly consistent. In farming, the fix was inside a
fertiliser factory. In transport, inside a refinery. Here it is inside a cylinder truck, a sewage
pipe, a mine vent and a sealed tank of gas on a wall. Almost never in the room where the carbon
actually leaves.

Which finally turns the question around. It is no longer what needs doing, or where. It is who in
India already owns the pipes, plants and networks where all of it has to happen.

# WORKING NOTES — not part of the answer

## Why this question exists

This is the fifth and last sector question, and the only one that is not a single industry. The
runway question set up five slices, of which the fifth is explicitly "everything left over" at 12.1
percent. This answer has to honour that: buildings, cooling, waste **and** gas leaks, or it is not
the same slice the reader was shown.

## The sector-share decision (8 Aug 2026)

Two datasets are in play across the sector questions and they disagree:

- **The runway (Q10)** uses Climate Watch / WRI 2023 via Our World in Data. India total ≈ 4.14 Bt
  CO₂e. Five slices summing to 100: power 37.5, industry 21.7, farming 19.5, transport 9.2,
  buildings+waste+leaks 12.1.
- **The sector files** use India's own BUR-4 inventory for 2020. Total 2,959 Mt without LULUCF. On
  that base the same five sectors come to roughly 39.6 / 21.3 / 13.7 / 10.1 / 12.1 and sum to ~96
  percent, because refineries and solid-fuel manufacture sit outside all five.

**Decision: the headline share in every sector answer comes from the runway.** Rows inside each table
show share *of the sector* (India's own count, which is authoritative for the split) and share *of
India* (that sector share multiplied by the runway headline). This keeps one denominator the reader
can trust and makes every table self-checking — the rows add to the headline.

The alternative — India's own base throughout — was rejected because it forces a visible sixth
"everything else" row that the runway never showed, and because it drops farming's headline to 13.7
percent, contradicting the 19.5 the reader already met.

## Corrections made to the earlier draft

1. **The 56 Mt "sewage" figure was wrong to present as one thing.** BUR-4's waste-water handling of
   55,893 Gg is 34,130 household and commercial plus 21,763 industrial. Two different problems, two
   different owners. Split into 2.3 and 2.5.
2. **A sixth sector was invented and removed.** An earlier draft carried "everything else 4.2%" as a
   separate row to force India's own base to 100. That contradicts the runway's five slices. Fixed by
   folding fugitive emissions into this slice, which is where the runway put them.
3. **Electricity was cut out entirely.** An earlier draft attributed 386 Mt of power-sector carbon to
   buildings via their 33.5 percent share of consumption. Removed on instruction — that carbon is
   counted in the power question and must not be double-told. Cooling therefore enters this answer
   through the **refrigerant**, which is a genuine direct emission of this sector, not through
   kilowatt-hours.

## Numbers found but deliberately not used

- **Buildings' electricity share.** CEA Growth Book 2025, FY2024-25: domestic 4,08,515 GWh (25.17%),
  commercial 1,35,163 GWh (8.33%) — 33.5% combined. Belongs to the power question.
- **Space-cooling electricity.** ICAP: ~135 TWh in 2017-18 rising to ~585 TWh by 2037-38, against
  total Indian generation of 2,059 TWh in FY2024-25. Belongs to the power question, and is the single
  strongest number available for the Energy Group payoff's demand case.
- **The appliance-versus-building finding.** India's S&L appliance star ratings avoided 57.46 Mt in
  FY2022-23 alone; UJALA LEDs avoid 39.30 Mt a year and 9,788 MW of peak. Against that, every
  building-code measure combined — ECBC 0.070 Mt, BEEP ~1.07 Mt, commercial star rating 0.13 Mt, ENS
  ~0.003 Mt — reaches about 1.3 Mt. Roughly 80:1. Only **365 ECBC-compliant commercial buildings**
  were registered in five years. All of this saving is *electricity*, so it sits in the power
  question — but it is the third confirmation of the upstream-lever pattern and should be used in the
  payoff.
- **The LED price crash.** ₹300–400 to about ₹70 through EESL bulk purchase, after which the market
  switched on its own. The cleanest available proof that changing one product's price beats
  persuading crores of buyers.

## Data problems flagged

- **BUR-4 prints "1071 MtCO2" for BEEP's 1,306 GWh of savings.** A factor-of-1000 typo; the real
  figure is ~1.07 Mt. Same class of error as BUR-4's PMKSY 555.3 Mt and MIDH 1,020.6 Mt, both of
  which remain excluded from all work.
- **Commercial/institutional fuel reads as 91 percent non-coking coal** (873,924 TJ in 2020), which
  is implausible for shops and offices and is almost certainly a residual allocation in the national
  energy balance. The total is used; the composition is flagged, not built on.
- **Halocarbon consumption is marked NA.** India does not estimate refrigerant leakage at all. This
  is the largest genuine unknown in the sector and the answer says so plainly.

## Seeds for the payoff question

1. **The upstream lever is now confirmed in five sectors out of five.** Neem-coated urea did 79
   percent of farming's cuts; ethanol blending did 85 percent of transport's; the appliance star
   rating beat the building code roughly 80 to 1. In every case the winning intervention sat inside a
   pipe or a factory that crores of people touch without choosing anything.
2. **Three boxes in this sector are marked "Not started" and total 141 Mt** — shops and offices, mine
   methane, factory water. Two of the three sit inside assets an infrastructure group already owns.
3. **585 TWh of cooling demand arriving at 22:36** is the demand-side case for storage, and it is the
   cleanest bridge from the sector questions to the Energy Group question.

Yes, it is bad — and the problem is the speed. Over a very long time, living things adjust: plants,
animals and even humans slowly change to suit the world around them, the way life has done for
millions of years. But that kind of change needs thousands of years, not forty. When the climate
shifts this fast, nature and people simply cannot keep up. And the warming does not just sit still —
it feeds itself. Heat melts the white ice at the poles, which uncovers dark ocean underneath; dark
water soaks up even more heat, which melts even more ice. Once a loop like this starts spinning, it
is very hard to stop, and it pushes the planet further and further toward real damage. So the change
is fast, it is dangerous, and it is speeding up on its own — which leaves one big question: who
started it?

*Sources: IPCC AR6 WG2 (limits to adaptation); IPCC AR6 WG1 SPM and SR1.5 (self-reinforcing feedbacks).*

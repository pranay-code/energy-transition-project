The answer begins with one simple fact: the Earth already cleans up after us, a little. Forests, the
soil, and the oceans all soak up some of the carbon we put out. The problem is how much we make.
We're now dumping far more carbon than nature can take back, so the leftover just keeps building up
in the air. To stop the warming, we have to cut down what we add until it fits what nature can soak
up. Once the two match, the pile stops growing. That balance has a name — "net zero."

So how do we get there? The big one is to burn a lot less coal, oil and gas, and to get our energy
from the sun, wind and water instead. Next, we look after our forests and oceans, since they do the
clean-up for free. And for the little bit of carbon we just can't avoid, we plant more trees or find
ways to trap it. Sounds simple enough, right? But no single country can fix this alone — the air is
shared by everyone, so this is truly a global challenge.

*Sources: IPCC AR6 WG3 SPM (net zero and mitigation pathways).*
